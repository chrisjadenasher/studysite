Periodic table

Every element, and the trends you need to know

Tap an element for its properties. Tap a group number or a period number to explore its compounds and trends for IGCSE, AS and A Level.

Relative atomic masses are given to one decimal place, as in the Cambridge AS & A Level Chemistry (9701) data booklet; elements with no stable isotopes have none listed. Other element data comes from standard data tables. Values can differ slightly between sources, and figures for the heaviest elements are estimates or unknown. Compound data follows the Cambridge IGCSE Chemistry (0620) and AS & A Level Chemistry (9701) syllabuses.

Practical toolkit

Uncertainties, gradients and significant figures

For Physics and Chemistry Papers 3 and 5. Use these to check your working after you have done it by hand, since the exam gives marks for the method you show.

Repeated readings

Mean of the readings, with the uncertainty taken as half the range.

Significant figures

Count the significant figures in a value, or round a result to a chosen number.

Combining uncertainties

For a result of the form k × ap × bq × …, percentage uncertainties are multiplied by the power and then added. The example finds g from a pendulum, g = 4π²L / T².

QuantityValue± absolutePower

Adding or subtracting quantities works differently: add the absolute uncertainties, not the percentages.

Gradient and worst acceptable line

One point per line: x, y, ± uncertainty in y. The best-fit line here is a least-squares fit; in the exam you draw yours by eye with points balanced either side.
